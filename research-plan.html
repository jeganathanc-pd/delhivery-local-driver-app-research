Research Goal

"Understand how drivers interact with the Delhivery Local app across their daily workflow — their mental models, pain points, workarounds, and unmet needs — to inform a full experience revamp that improves fulfillment rate, reduces cancellations, and increases driver retention."

Research Questions

  1. How do drivers structure their working day, and where does the Delhivery Local app fit alongside other apps (Porter, Rapido, Uber)?
  2. What is the driver's mental model of the app's core flow (broadcast → accept → pickup → deliver → mark complete)?
  3. Where do drivers experience friction, confusion, or anxiety during the order lifecycle?
  4. How do drivers discover and use secondary features (Trip Radar, Slots, Incentives, Performance, Earnings)?
  5. What workarounds have drivers built to compensate for app limitations?
  6. Why do drivers cancel orders, and what conditions lead to repeated cancellations?
  7. What drives a driver to prefer a competitor app over Delhivery Local?

Project Context

Product

Delhivery Local — Intracity delivery driver app

Live for: 2 years

Cities: 6 (Ahmedabad, Jaipur, Delhi NCR, Bangalore, Mumbai, Hyderabad)

Total registered drivers: ~79,000+

Daily active drivers: ~35,000–38,000

Weekly active drivers: ~29,000 (grew from ~8K in April)

Daily orders: ~18,000–20,000

Daily broadcasts: ~450,000–510,000

Business Metrics — Current vs Target

  • Fulfillment rate: 54.5% → target >70% (gap: -15.5 pts)
  • Pickup under 15 min: ~62% → target >70% (gap: -8 pts)
  • P80 pickup time: 21 min → target <15 min
  • Acceptance rate: ~5% → needs fundamental improvement
  • Post-assignment cancellation: 20.9% → needs reduction
  • CM1 (contribution margin): -10.35% → target positive

Driver Profile

  • Gig workers (multi-app: Porter, Rapido, Uber)
  • Vehicle types: 2W, 3W, mini-3W, Tata Ace, 8ft pickup
  • Age: 25–50 years
  • Tech literacy: Low to medium
  • Devices: Mid-range Android phones
  • Avg login hours: 5.6–6.1 hrs/day
  • Avg deliveries/driver/day: 0.37
  • Avg earnings/driver/day: ₹123
  • 80% of orders are COD (cash on delivery)

Why the Revamp?

  • Old UI with scattered feature entry points (~5 CTAs on homepage)
  • Features added organically without cohesive IA
  • Migrating to new design system "TARMAC"
  • Want experience-level improvement, not just UI refresh
  • Data-backed: 95% of broadcasts ignored, 21% post-assignment cancellations, 54.5% fulfillment (target >70%), negative margins in all cities

Core App Flow

Receive broadcast → Accept order (before expiry) → Navigate to pickup → Pick up order → Navigate to drop → Deliver order → Collect cash (80% COD) → Mark delivered

PM Interview Questions (26 questions)

On the Fulfillment Problem (54.5% vs 70% target)

  1. What's your current hypothesis for why fulfillment is so low? What have you already tried?
  2. Which part of the problem do you think is solvable by the app vs. needs ops/pricing changes?
  3. Is there an internal breakdown of that 54.5%? How much is "no driver found" vs "driver cancelled" vs "customer cancelled"?
  4. The data shows more drivers (8K→29K) didn't improve fulfillment. Were you aware? What's the theory?
  5. What's the current broadcast logic? How does the system decide which driver to ping first?
  6. Is there a plan to change the broadcast algorithm? What variables does it consider?

On Post-Assignment Cancellations (21% of orders)

  1. Are you aware that 21% of orders die after a driver accepts? Is this tracked at leadership level?
  2. "Driver asked me to cancel" is 19% of cancellations. Is the cancellation penalty system under review?
  3. What happens to a driver's account when they cancel? What's the penalty? How harsh is it?
  4. 23% of cancellations are "distance surprise" — does the broadcast show straight-line or actual route distance?
  5. 20% are vehicle mismatch — is the consumer selecting vehicle type? Is the system auto-assigning?

On Star/Non-Star System

  1. What exactly are the criteria for Star status? Can you share the formula?
  2. Do drivers know they're Star/Non-Star? Is it communicated in the app?
  3. Do Star drivers get priority on broadcasts or better orders?
  4. What's the Star → Non-Star churn rate? How quickly do Stars fall?

On Slots/MBG (Declining)

  1. Slot contribution is dropping (9.5% → 7.5%). Are you worried about this?
  2. What's the guaranteed amount per day for MBG drivers? Is it competitive vs. Porter earnings?
  3. Are Slot drivers better because they're committed, or because the best drivers opt into Slots?
  4. Any plans to change the Slot structure (shorter hours, partial exclusivity)?

On Competition & Multi-App Behavior

  1. How much of our driver base do you estimate is also on Porter/Rapido?
  2. Do you have any data on what Porter/Rapido pays per order vs. Delhivery?
  3. Is there anything in the pipeline to make Delhivery the "primary" app for drivers?

On the Revamp Scope

  1. What does success look like for this revamp? Is it a specific fulfillment number?
  2. Are there any features/changes already decided that I should factor into my research?
  3. What's off the table? (Things you've explored and rejected)
  4. From your perspective, what are the top 3 problems the app redesign should solve?
Ops Team Interview Questions (30 questions)

On Driver Behavior (Ground Reality)

  1. What are the top 5 complaints you hear from drivers every day?
  2. When drivers call support, what are the most common reasons?
  3. Do drivers complain about the broadcast/order notification? (not seeing it, not enough info, etc.)
  4. What do drivers say when asked why they cancelled an order?
  5. Have you heard drivers say the pickup was farther than expected after accepting?
  6. Do drivers openly talk about using Porter/Rapido simultaneously?

On the "Gaming" Problem (19% — "Driver asked me to cancel")

  1. Are you aware drivers ask customers to cancel instead of cancelling themselves? How widespread?
  2. Why do they do it — is it the penalty? What's the exact penalty they face?
  3. What does a driver's day look like when they get suspended for too many cancellations?
  4. Have you seen drivers develop "tricks" to avoid the penalty system?

On Pickup Issues

  1. How often do drivers complain about waiting at pickup? What do they say?
  2. Do merchants have the package ready when the driver arrives? What's the typical delay reason?
  3. Data shows 50% of "driver issue" cancellations are verified refusals at pickup. What's happening there?
  4. Are there specific merchants or locations that drivers hate going to? Why?
  5. Do drivers complain about wrong addresses or unreachable pickup locations?

On the Night Problem (fulfillment 10-25% after 9 PM)

  1. Do you have drivers who work nights? What's different about them?
  2. What's the demand like at night? What type of orders come in?
  3. Have there been safety incidents at night that made drivers avoid it?
  4. Is there any night-shift premium or incentive?

On Onboarding & Retention

  1. How do new drivers learn to use the app? Is there a training process?
  2. How long does it take a new driver to become productive?
  3. Why do drivers leave Delhivery? What are the top churn reasons?
  4. What makes a driver come back after going inactive?

On City Differences

  1. Ahmedabad/Jaipur perform much better than Bangalore/Delhi/Mumbai. Why do you think that is?
  2. Is Porter/Rapido less dominant in Ahmedabad/Jaipur?
  3. Are there city-specific operational differences (incentive structures, ops team size)?

On Operations vs App Problems

  1. In your experience, which driver problems are caused by the app vs caused by operations/processes?
  2. If you could fix ONE thing that isn't an app problem but kills fulfillment, what would it be?
  3. What operational interventions have worked to improve driver performance?
  4. Are there manual processes you do that should be automated in the app?

Phase 1: Field Research with Drivers

Week 2: Aug 11–15 Week 3: Aug 18–22

The core of the research. Three complementary methods to build a complete picture.

Primary Method

Method 1: Contextual Inquiry / Ride-Alongs

What: Shadow drivers during their actual working hours with an ops team member as translator.

Why: See real behavior — how they hold the phone while riding, when they check the app, what confuses them, what workarounds they've developed.

Duration: 2–3 hours per driver

Sample: 8–10 drivers

What to observe:

  • Physical setup (phone mount, how they manage cash, vehicle condition)
  • App usage patterns during idle/waiting time
  • Decision-making process when broadcast comes in (accept/reject criteria)
  • Navigation behavior (do they use in-app nav, Google Maps, or memory?)
  • Pickup and drop-off process (COD cash handling, OTP, proof of delivery)
  • How they switch between multiple apps
  • Emotional states throughout the journey (stress points, satisfaction moments)
  • Interactions with customers and merchants

Questions to ask during ride-along:

  • "Walk me through your morning when you start working"
  • "How do you decide which orders to accept?"
  • "When was the last time you were frustrated with this app?"
  • "Show me how you check your earnings"
  • "What do you do when there are no orders?"
  • "Why do you sometimes cancel an order after accepting?"
  • "What do you like better about [Porter/Rapido]?"

Data-driven questions (ranked by impact):

  • "Why do you ask the customer to cancel instead of cancelling yourself?" (18.86% of cancellations)
  • "Has the pickup ever been farther than you expected after accepting?" (12.61% — distance surprise)
  • "Do you ever accept orders while finishing another delivery on a different app?" (5,794 moved-away cancellations)
  • "What makes you decide a package won't fit in your vehicle?" (20% vehicle mismatch)
  • "What happens when you reach the pickup and the package isn't ready?" (12 min avg wait, 50% verified refusal)
  • "What would the broadcast need to show you for you to accept/reject faster?" (only 5% acceptance, 14 broadcasts per order in Bangalore)
  • "Do you know what your Star/Non-Star status is? Does it matter to you?"
  • "What time do you usually stop for the day? Why?" (fulfillment collapses after 9 PM)
Secondary Method

Method 2: In-Office Usability Testing

What: Bring drivers to office and give them task-based scenarios on the current app.

Why: Structured way to identify specific usability issues per screen/flow.

Duration: 45–60 minutes per session

Sample: 5–6 drivers (different from ride-along participants)

Task Scenarios:

  1. "Show me how you would check your earnings from last week"
  2. "You want to work in a specific time slot tomorrow — how would you do that?"
  3. "Show me where you can see available trips nearby"
  4. "How would you check if you're close to earning an incentive today?"
  5. "Walk me through what happens from when you get an order to marking it delivered"
  6. "Show me how you'd check your performance rating"

Protocol: Think-aloud (translated by ops person). Record screen + audio with consent.

Supplementary

Method 3: Quick Intercept Surveys

What: Short 5-minute structured questionnaire at driver hubs/hotspots.

Why: Get broader quantitative signals on satisfaction, feature awareness, and competitor usage.

Sample: 20–30 drivers (can be administered by ops team via Google Form)

Survey Topics:

  • Overall app satisfaction (1–5 scale)
  • Feature awareness checklist (do they know Trip Radar, Slots, Incentives exist?)
  • Top 3 frustrations (open-ended)
  • Other apps they use and why
  • What one thing they'd change about the app
  • How they learned to use the app

Phase 2: Synthesis & Analysis

Week 3–4: Aug 22–29

Transform raw observations into actionable design insights.

1. Affinity Mapping

Cluster all observations, quotes, and notes into themes.

Example themes:

  • Navigation confusion
  • Cash management stress
  • Feature discovery issues
  • Broadcast decision-making anxiety
  • Earnings transparency gaps
  • Multi-app juggling patterns

2. Journey Mapping

Map the end-to-end driver journey with these layers:

  • Stages: Start day → Wait for orders → Accept → Pickup → Transit → Deliver → Review earnings → End day
  • Actions: What they do at each stage
  • Touchpoints: App screens, calls, physical interactions
  • Pain points: Frustrations and friction
  • Emotions: Stress, confusion, satisfaction, anxiety
  • Opportunities: Where design can help

3. Behavioral Personas

Create 2–3 driver personas based on behavior patterns (not just demographics).

Data-suggested persona axes:

  • The Committed Star — Logs 6-7 hrs, accepts 9-15% of broadcasts, delivers 0.8-1.1 orders/day, earns ₹200-307/day. Delhivery is primary app.
  • The Multi-App Juggler — Logs 5+ hrs but 0.24 deliveries/day on Delhivery. 0-1.2% acceptance rate. Receives 300+ broadcasts/week, takes almost none. Porter/Rapido is primary; Delhivery is backup.
  • The Ghost Driver — Logs <1 hr daily (4-5K drivers do this). Tests the app, finds nothing worth accepting, leaves. May be new, disengaged, or churning.
  • The Slot Committer — Opted into MBG. Online 8 hrs exclusively. Takes what comes. Better at short distances. Declining segment — validate why.

4. Opportunity Scoring

CriteriaScale
Frequency (how often does this happen?)1–5
Severity (how painful when it happens?)1–5
Business Impact (does fixing this move our metrics?)1–5

Score = Frequency × Severity × Business Impact

5. Insight Statements

"We observed [behavior], because [underlying reason], which means [implication for design]."

Example: "We observed drivers checking Porter between Delhivery broadcasts, because they fear idle time means lost income, which means our idle state needs to proactively surface earning opportunities."

Tips & Considerations

Managing the Language Barrier

  • Brief your translator beforehand — share key questions in advance
  • Use visual methods — "show me" rather than "tell me"
  • Create a simple observation template — note body language, hesitations, tap patterns
  • Card sorting exercises — visual and less language-dependent
  • Record everything — get detailed translations later

Ride-Along Best Practices

  • Don't interrupt — observe first, ask during natural breaks
  • Note the environment — noise, weather, traffic affect usability
  • Pay attention to what they do between orders
  • Ask "why" gently — "I noticed you did X, can you tell me why?"
  • Document their phone setup — brightness, notifications, other apps

Things to Watch For (Data-Backed)

  • COD friction: 80% of orders are COD — observe cash handling pain points
  • Broadcast decision-making: 95% of broadcasts are ignored. System pings 9-14 drivers before one accepts. Watch: do they see the notification? What info makes them decide? How fast can they process it?
  • Multi-app switching: 5,794 cancellations from drivers moving AWAY from pickup (doing another app's order). Watch: how many apps are open? Which is "primary"?
  • Distance surprise: 23% of cancellations are because actual route is longer than expected. Watch: what does the broadcast show vs. what navigation reveals after accepting
  • Pickup waiting: 12 min average wait at pickup (unpaid). 50% of "driver issue" = verified refusal at pickup. Watch: what happens when they arrive and package isn't ready
  • Night behavior: Fulfillment collapses to 10-25% after 9 PM. Do they stop because of safety? Fatigue? No demand on other apps either?
  • Slot/MBG perception: Declining (9.5% → 7.5%). 8-hr exclusivity + no-cancel rules — do they find this too restrictive?
  • Star awareness: Do drivers know their tier? Does it motivate them? Do they know what improves it?
