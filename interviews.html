Finding Insight Opportunity

The swipes drivers use every trip — swipe to start, swipe to mark arrived, swipe to mark delivered — often don't register or don't save. The app freezes on a loading screen at the end of a trip, and sometimes logs the driver out in the middle of a shift. Every time this happens, the driver has to call the ops team to close the trip for them. The app is failing at its most basic job, and the ops team has become the manual backup.

Came up in all 4 interviews — the most widespread problem we found.

2. The distance shown is not the real distance

When an order comes in, the app shows a short straight-line distance, but the actual road distance is much longer. Drivers accept based on the wrong number, feel cheated, and start ignoring future orders. For a 3-wheeler driver the gap is 3–4 km, which works out to roughly ₹90 lost on every order.

Raised separately by 3 drivers, and now we have the numbers.

3. Drivers get blamed for cancellations that aren't theirs

When a driver is given two orders at once, one auto-cancels and the driver is blamed. If the customer cancels, it still counts against the driver. Even going offline can push an order to them that they're then forced to cancel. And some roads are closed to 3-wheelers (like Brigade Road), but the app doesn't warn them, so the customer cancels and the driver takes the hit. On top of all this, drivers can't even see who actually caused a cancellation. These marks decide whether a driver qualifies for guaranteed-income slots, so it directly affects their earnings.

Raised by all 3 drivers — a major trust issue.

4. Drivers pay for costs the company should cover

Drivers cover a lot of unpaid work: the distance travelled to reach a pickup (slots only pay for the drop distance), 5–20 minutes waiting at the shop, the effort of loading and unloading, and extra distance caused by wrong addresses. Even when waiting time is paid, the app doesn't show when or how much. Porter sets the bar here — it pays for any extra travel over 1 km and shows a clear breakdown of everything.

Came up in every driver interview.

9. No option to get paid for loading and unloading

Loading and unloading is unavoidable, takes time, and pays nothing. If a driver refuses to do it, the customer just cancels ("I'll cancel, you go") and the driver gets nothing. Porter turns this into paid work: the customer can add a loading charge that goes to the driver, high-effort orders are tagged so drivers can see them upfront, and it shows clearly during the trip. One 3-wheeler driver specifically asked us to build this.

From Loknath's interview — a proven feature we're missing.

10. Drivers are gaming the Trip Radar surge

Drivers have learned that if they ignore an order, it moves to Trip Radar and the price slowly goes up. So they deliberately skip long-distance orders and wait for the price to climb before accepting. One driver said all his friends do this. Because the price increase is predictable, the system is quietly teaching drivers to reject orders first.

From Loknath's interview — inflates payouts and hurts fulfillment.

11. Too few orders for 3-wheelers, plus weak support

A 3-wheeler driver gets about 1 order an hour from Delhivery versus a steady stream from Porter, so Delhivery is only a backup for them. Support made it worse: waits over 10 minutes, calls cut short because the agent didn't speak Kannada, an email that was never answered, and no way to update bank details in the app — forcing a trip to the office for a tiny change. There's also no emergency or SOS button.

From Loknath's interview — order volume, support, and safety gaps.

5. Wrong pickup and drop locations

The app sometimes points to the wrong place, so the driver travels extra, the customer blames them, and no one pays for the wasted distance. Drivers get around it by calling the customer or going off landmarks they remember.

Both drivers mentioned it; Porter pays for this extra travel.

6. No way to see where the demand is

Drivers move to busy areas on their own, using a mental map built from experience on other apps. They want the app to show busy zones and higher-pay areas, the way Rapido does. Experienced drivers manage; new drivers have nothing to go on.

Both drivers; the ops team confirms drivers rely on guesswork.

7. Support is easier to reach for some drivers than others

Drivers who happen to know someone on the ops team can skip the queue; everyone else goes through the slow general support line. And support is hardest to reach during a live trip — exactly when a problem is costing the driver money.

Seen in the ops process and in Murugan's live-trip experience.

8. New drivers get no guidance

There's no in-app walkthrough for new drivers. They work it out on their own, and anything confusing turns into a support call. Training videos only help about half of them; the rest learn by doing their first few orders.

Both the ops team and drivers agree on this learn-by-doing gap.

What's Working

Slots keep 2-wheeler drivers loyal

Both 2-wheeler drivers value the guaranteed-income slots, and one of them (M Gowda) earns more from Delhivery specifically because of them. It's a real advantage over other apps when it works. The catch: lower-tier and 3-wheeler drivers can't access slots at all.

Drivers genuinely like Trip Radar

A 3-wheeler driver brought up Trip Radar on his own as one of the few features he really likes, because it lets him choose trips himself instead of waiting to be offered one. Porter has since copied the idea. It's worth investing in — and worth protecting from the surge-gaming problem noted above.

Earnings are clear once drivers learn them

The earnings page makes sense to drivers. The only early confusion is around cash orders — knowing what to collect from the customer — and that sorts itself out after a couple of orders.

Drivers who work deliver on time

Active drivers hit 98–100% on-time delivery. So the real problem isn't whether drivers can do the job — it's getting them to accept orders in the first place. The interview evidence matches what the data shows.

Top Requested Features

1. Pay for loading and unloading

Let the customer add a loading charge that goes to the driver, and flag these orders upfront so drivers know the effort involved. Drivers do this work for free today, and Porter already pays for it.

2. Show where the orders are

A live map of busy areas and higher-pay zones inside the app, so drivers know where to go instead of guessing. Both drivers asked for something like Rapido's demand map.

3. Good help and support

Support that's easy to reach and actually helps — short wait times, agents who speak the driver's language, and quick answers during a live trip. Today drivers wait over 10 minutes, get calls cut short over a language mismatch, and some can only get help if they personally know someone on the team.

A Tension to Flag

Drivers disagree on splitting scooter and bike orders. One driver (M Gowda) wants the app to tell scooters and bikes apart, because oversized packages keep landing on his bike. Another (Murugan) is against it — customers prefer scooters, so bike-only drivers would end up with fewer orders. There's no clean win here; it's a trade-off that needs balancing.

Trip Radar is loved and abused at the same time. Loknath calls Trip Radar one of the few genuinely useful features — but the same feature is what lets drivers game the surge (skip long orders, wait for the price to rise, then accept). We can't improve one without addressing the other, so they have to be designed together.

Mapped to the PM's Lens

App-solvable
  • Make the swipe-to-complete reliable, add a tap option, and fix trips that freeze at the end or log drivers out
  • Show the real driving distance for the driver's vehicle, not a straight-line estimate
  • Show where demand and surge pricing are high inside the app
  • Show why an order was cancelled and who it was counted against
  • Show the real earnings per km on the order card
  • Add an SOS / emergency button
  • Let drivers edit their own bank details
  • Break down wait-time pay clearly in the wallet
  • Add in-app guidance and tips for new drivers
  • Show a "package ready" signal from the merchant before the driver arrives
Ops-solvable
  • Make support easier to reach and fair for every driver
  • Provide support agents in the driver's own language (e.g. Kannada) and set clear response times
Economics-solvable
  • Match Porter's pay, especially on long-distance orders
  • Pay drivers for loading and unloading effort
  • Pay for distance travelled to pickup and time spent waiting
  • Pay for extra distance when the route is longer than shown
  • Set incentives drivers can actually reach, not just high-volume targets
  • Watch new zero-commission competitors like Towner
  • Benchmark earnings against the government minimum wage per hour
System-solvable
  • Correctly attribute who caused a cancellation, including 3W road restrictions
  • Make surge pricing unpredictable so drivers can't game Trip Radar by waiting
  • Route around roads that specific vehicle types can't use
  • Don't assign orders while a driver is logging out
  • Match package size to the right vehicle type (3W vs Tata Ace)
  • Assign orders based on a driver's past trips
  • Offer return-trip / go-home orders at the end of the day

Summary of the Research

Most of what drivers told us traces back to two things. First, the app lets them down at the moments they can least afford it: the swipe to complete a trip, the distance it quotes, the way a cancellation gets pinned on them. When that happens they stop trusting the app and start managing around it, calling ops and going by their own read of the situation. Second, and this is the part that keeps them one foot out the door, the money is better elsewhere. Porter and the new zero-commission Towner hand out more orders, pay for the waiting and loading Delhivery ignores, and are honest about what a driver will take home. That is why Delhivery ends up as the backup app they open when nothing else is going, and why they will sit on the Trip Radar surge until the fare is worth their time. Fix the reliability, pay people fairly for the work they are already doing, and send more volume to the drivers earning the least, and you will see acceptance and cancellations shift before any new feature ever ships.
